This policy describes how content is produced, reviewed, and corrected on ToolScope. It applies to every article, comparison, and guide we publish, in English and in Arabic.

## Independence

- We do not accept payment for reviews, verdicts, or rankings.
- We do not allow vendors, PR agencies, or affiliates to preview, edit, or veto editorial content.
- If a vendor provides a free license or account for testing, we disclose it in the review.

## Scores and verdicts

- A score is only published when testing described in our [methodology](/en/testing-methodology) is complete.
- Until then, articles carry an explicit "rating pending" state instead of a number.
- Verdicts are written by a human editor. We do not generate reviews with AI and publish them unreviewed.

## Sources and evidence

- Claims about product behavior are backed by hands-on use, official documentation, or clearly attributed public sources.
- When we cannot verify something — pricing, a feature, a claim — we mark it as unverified rather than guessing.
- Screenshots and evidence in reviews come from our own test setups unless stated otherwise.

## Corrections

Mistakes get fixed, visibly:

1. Factual errors are corrected and the article shows an "Updated" date.
2. Substantive changes to a verdict or recommendation are noted in the article itself.
3. Readers can report errors through the [contact page](/en/contact); we respond to every correction request.

## Sponsored content

We may publish clearly labeled sponsored or partner content in the future, under these rules:

- It will be visually and verbally distinct from editorial content.
- It will never use the review template, the score system, or our recommendations format.
- The sponsor will be named in the title area, not hidden in a footer.

## Demonstration content

While the publication launches, some articles are marked **Demonstration content**. These exist to show article structure and layout. They are excluded from search engines and make no factual claims about products.

## Translation policy

English is the primary publication language. Arabic articles are edited as independent editorial work — not machine-dumped translations. An Arabic article may be a translation, a summary, or an original piece; each is labeled accordingly.

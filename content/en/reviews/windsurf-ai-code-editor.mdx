## What makes a flow-first editor different

Most AI-first editors compete on how much the AI can do — how many files it can rewrite, how long its context window stretches. Windsurf competes on how little the AI disrupts your train of thought. The difference sounds subtle until you spend an afternoon coding with both approaches.

This review examines whether that design philosophy holds up in practice, or whether it is just a polite way of saying "less capable."

## The editing experience

Three qualities define Windsurf's daily feel:

1. **Inline suggestions over chat.** The default mode prioritises completions that appear where you're already typing, rather than routing you to a sidebar conversation.
2. **Lightweight architecture.** Startup is noticeably faster than Electron-heavy rivals, and memory usage stays lower during long sessions.
3. **Gradual disclosure.** Advanced AI features (multi-file edits, codebase search) exist but don't compete for attention until you invoke them.

## Where it falls short

The flow-first philosophy has trade-offs. Multi-file operations feel less polished than in editors that were built around them. The extension marketplace is growing but cannot match the VS Code ecosystem. And the AI's context window, while adequate for single-file work, sometimes misses relevant code in other modules.

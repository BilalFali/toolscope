## Why local-first matters

Most note apps are rental agreements: your writing lives in a vendor's database, exported on their terms. Obsidian's model is the opposite — a vault is a folder of Markdown files. That one decision shapes everything: search is instant, migration is trivial, and the worst case is that you keep using the files with any text editor.

## The daily-work experience

The core loop — write, link, revisit — is fast. Backlinks and the graph view make connections discoverable rather than aspirational. Plugins extend the editor into task management, spaced repetition, or publishing, and the good ones feel native rather than bolted on.

## Where the friction is

Sync is the honest weak point: the official service costs extra, and the do-it-yourself routes (Git, iCloud, Syncthing) each carry caveats on mobile. Collaboration is possible but clumsy. And the plugin ecosystem's freedom means two installs of Obsidian can behave very differently.

## Bottom line

For individual knowledge work with a decade-long horizon, the local-first model is worth real money, and Obsidian's implementation of it is the most complete we've found in research — pending the hands-on cycle that would turn that into a score. For teams and hosted-everything preferences, [Notion](/en/compare/obsidian-vs-notion) is worth comparing directly.

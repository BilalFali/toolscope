## The problem Tailscale exists to solve

Traditional VPNs ask you to think like a network administrator: subnets, gateways, certificate lifetimes. For a five-person team with three cloud servers and a home lab, that overhead is disproportionate to the actual need — which is simply "our services should be reachable only by us, from anywhere."

Tailscale's model flips the setup: devices authenticate with an identity provider, and the mesh handles addressing and encryption. You express policy as rules about identities, not firewall arcs.

## What we intend to test

A full review under our [testing methodology](/en/testing-methodology) would cover:

1. **Time-to-first-connection** on a clean machine, per platform.
2. **Stability** across two weeks of daily use, including sleep/wake cycles and network switches.
3. **Throughput** between two representative nodes, measured, with hardware stated.
4. **Access control experience**: writing and revising ACLs as a non-network-engineer.
5. **Exit paths**: what happens when the coordination service is unreachable, and what self-hosting actually requires.

## Why no score yet

Each of those tests takes time, and some — stability especially — cannot be compressed. We would rather publish a review late than invent a number. When testing completes, the verdict and score appear here with the evidence described.

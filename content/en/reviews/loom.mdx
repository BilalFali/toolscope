## What Loom actually is

Loom is a screen-and-webcam recording tool built for asynchronous communication — record once, share a link, let the recipient watch on their own time instead of scheduling a call. It's now owned by Atlassian, which is relevant mainly for the Enterprise tier's Atlassian Guard Standard integration and Jira/Confluence-adjacent positioning.

## The free tier is usable, with a real ceiling

Loom's Starter plan is genuinely free, covers up to 50 workspace members, and doesn't cap meeting-recording length — but screen recordings specifically are capped at 5 minutes, and the account is limited to 25 total videos. For quick bug reports or short walkthroughs, that's workable. For anything resembling a tutorial or a longer product demo, the 5-minute wall is a real constraint, not a soft one.

## Where the AI tier changes the calculation

Loom's pricing has a notable structural choice: AI features (auto-titles, summaries, chapters, CTAs, task extraction, filler-word and silence removal, editing by transcript) are not bundled into the base $18/month Business tier — they require stepping up to Business + AI at $24/user/month. That's a $6/user/month premium specifically for AI, on top of the jump from free to paid. Teams evaluating Loom against competitors should price the AI tier specifically, not the base Business tier, if AI features are the reason they're considering Loom at all.

## Enterprise: built for IT, not individuals

The Enterprise tier (custom pricing) adds SSO, SCIM, Atlassian Guard Standard, unlimited spaces, custom data retention, and a 99.95% uptime SLA — all enterprise procurement checklist items rather than features an individual or small team would notice day to day. This tier exists for organizations with compliance requirements, not as the "better" version of Loom for everyone.

## Who should consider it

If your team already works asynchronously and the main value is a searchable, shareable recording with a transcript, Loom's free tier is worth trying before paying for anything. If you specifically want AI summaries and auto-titling, budget for the $24/user/month tier, not the $18 one — a distinction that's easy to miss when comparing headline prices.

## What would change our verdict

There is no score on this page because hands-on testing hasn't happened yet. A real evaluation would test actual recording quality at each resolution tier, measure how accurate the AI-generated summaries and chapters really are against a recording's actual content, and confirm whether the 5-minute free-tier cap is announced clearly before a recording is cut off mid-walkthrough.

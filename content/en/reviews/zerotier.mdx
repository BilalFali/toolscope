## A different billing axis, not just a different brand

ZeroTier solves the same core problem as Tailscale — private, encrypted connectivity between devices without traditional VPN gateways and subnets — but structures its pricing around **devices**, not **users**. That single difference determines which tool's free tier actually fits a given team better than any feature comparison does.

## What the free tier actually covers

ZeroTier's Personal tier is free with up to 10 devices on a single network, with no login or user-count limit stated on the official pricing page. That's meaningfully different from a per-user free tier: a single person managing 8 home-lab devices fits comfortably, while a 12-person team sharing 6 total servers also fits — the constraint is the device count, not the headcount.

The trade-off is the single-network cap: if you need to segment traffic across genuinely separate networks (say, a home lab and a side project with different trust boundaries), the free tier doesn't accommodate that without upgrading.

## Paid tiers and what they add

Essential ($18/month) includes 10 devices and adds API access, an AI agent, basic access control, and 30-day audit logs. Scale ($179/month) raises the device allowance to 100 and removes the per-network limit. Both tiers charge for devices beyond the included count — $2.00/device/month on Essential, $1.80/device/month on Scale — which matters for teams whose device count grows past the plan's included allowance.

## Security tiers worth knowing about

ZeroTier's Enterprise and Quantum tiers (both custom-priced) explicitly state FIPS 140-3 validated cryptography, with Quantum adding post-quantum cryptography (CNSA 2.0 compliant) and air-gapped deployment support. These are relevant only if you have a specific, named compliance requirement — not a general recommendation, and not something the lower tiers claim.

## Self-hosting: a real but vague option

ZeroTier's pricing page describes deploying "within your own infrastructure for full control and flexibility" but doesn't publish a specific self-hosted price, device limit, or plan name. If self-hosting is a hard requirement, verify the current terms directly in ZeroTier's own documentation rather than assuming a figure — we could not find one stated plainly on the official pricing page.

## How it compares to Tailscale

See our full [Tailscale vs ZeroTier comparison](/en/compare/tailscale-vs-zerotier) for the side-by-side pricing table. The short version: Tailscale's free tier is more generous for a small number of *people* with many devices each; ZeroTier's is more generous for a fixed *device* count regardless of who manages them.

## What would change our verdict

There is no score on this page because hands-on testing hasn't happened yet. A real evaluation would time setup on a clean machine, measure throughput between two representative nodes, and document what happens at the exact 10-device free-tier boundary — including whether the 11th device fails gracefully or silently.

## Two different answers to "where do my notes live"

Obsidian and Notion both help you write, link, and organize notes, but they start from opposite premises. Obsidian's unit is a plain-text file on your own disk; Notion's unit is a block in a hosted, shared workspace. That one decision — file vs. database, local vs. hosted — explains most of the practical differences below.

## Pricing, compared directly

| | Obsidian | Notion |
|---|---|---|
| Core product | Free, unlimited, no sign-up required | Free plan with limits (e.g. capped AI trial, basic forms) |
| First paid tier | Sync: $4/user/month (annual) | Plus: $10/member/month |
| Collaboration | Requires Sync add-on; co-editing is a paid feature | Native at every tier, including Free |
| Top individual-relevant tier | Publish: $8/site/month, for sharing notes on the web | Business: $20/member/month, adds SSO and private teamspaces |
| Commercial use | $50/user/year, separate from Sync/Publish | Included in per-member pricing |

Source: [Obsidian pricing](https://obsidian.md/pricing) and [Notion pricing](https://www.notion.com/pricing) as published on 2026-10-27.

The structural point: Obsidian unbundles storage (free, local) from sync and collaboration (paid add-ons). Notion bundles storage, sync, and collaboration into one per-member price from the start — you're never choosing "do I pay for sync," because hosting is the product.

## Ownership and portability

Obsidian notes are Markdown files in a folder; you can open, grep, or migrate them with any text editor, with or without Obsidian installed. Notion's content lives in a hosted database; exporting is supported, but a large workspace with nested databases and relations does not reduce to plain files as cleanly. If long-term data portability is a hard requirement, this is the most consequential difference in this comparison — more than any single pricing line.

## Collaboration

This is Notion's clearest structural advantage: shared, permissioned, real-time editing is native at every tier, including Free. Obsidian can approximate collaboration through its paid Sync add-on or third-party sync tools, but co-editing is not the product's design center — it was built for an individual's local vault first.

## Who should pick which

If your priority is owning your notes as portable files for a decade-plus horizon, and you're comfortable choosing your own sync method, Obsidian's model is built around exactly that. If you need a team to share, structure, and collaborate on content today — databases, permissions, shared workspaces — Notion's hosted model removes setup work Obsidian would otherwise require you to assemble yourself (sync, publishing, access control).

## What would make this comparison more useful

A hands-on follow-up would test sync reliability across devices for both tools, measure how each performs with a large note/database count (10,000+ items), and document actual export fidelity when migrating a real workspace out of Notion. Until that cycle runs, treat this as a pricing and structural comparison, not a performance verdict.

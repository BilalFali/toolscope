## The question underneath "which app should I use"

Before comparing feature lists, answer a more basic question: **where do you want your notes to physically live, and for how long do you expect to still be reading them?** Note apps split fundamentally into local-first (your notes are files you own) and hosted (your notes live in the provider's database). That decision matters more over a ten-year horizon than any single feature comparison.

## Step 1: Decide your ownership requirement

- **If you want files you can open with any text editor, in ten years, regardless of whether the company still exists** — look at local-first, plain-Markdown tools: Obsidian, Logseq.
- **If you need real-time collaboration with other people, and are comfortable with your notes living in a vendor's hosted database** — look at Notion or similar hosted workspaces.

This is the single highest-leverage decision in the category. Everything else — plugins, AI features, price — is secondary to it.

## Step 2: Separate the free core product from paid add-ons

Several of these tools unbundle the free core experience from paid extras in different ways:

- Obsidian's app is free and unlimited; sync and publishing are separate paid add-ons.
- Notion bundles hosting and basic collaboration into its Free tier, with paid tiers adding capacity and admin controls.
- Joplin includes sync in the core product, but you pick and often pay for your own sync backend.

Work out what you'll actually need — solo use with occasional device sync, or full team collaboration — before comparing sticker prices across these different bundling models.

## Step 3: Match the editing model to how you actually think

- **Document-first** (Obsidian): long-form writing with backlinks between full notes.
- **Outliner/block-first** (Logseq, and Notion's blocks): everything nests as bullets or blocks, which some people find clarifying and others find restrictive.

Neither is objectively better. A person who thinks in structured outlines will fight a document-first tool, and vice versa. This is worth testing directly, not assuming from a feature list.

## Step 4: Check what "free" actually includes, not just that it exists

"Free" means different things across this category — a usage-limited trial, a genuinely unlimited core app, or a free tier capped by collaborator count. Read the current official pricing page for any tool you're seriously considering; third-party pricing summaries (including comparison articles like this one) can lag behind real vendor changes.

## Step 5: Run a real evaluation before migrating your notes

Migrating years of notes is costly to undo. Before committing:

1. **Import a real sample** of your existing notes, not a fresh empty vault/workspace.
2. **Use the app for actual daily work for at least a week** — not a five-minute tour.
3. **Test the export path on day one**, before you have thousands of notes trapped in a format you'd need to extract later.

## Common mistakes

1. **Choosing based on a feature list without testing the editing model against how you actually think.**
2. **Ignoring collaboration needs until a second person needs access** — retrofitting sharing onto a local-first tool is more work than planning for it up front.
3. **Not testing the export path before committing years of notes** to a format or service you haven't verified you can leave.
